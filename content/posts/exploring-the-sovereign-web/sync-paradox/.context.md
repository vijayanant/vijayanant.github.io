## 1. The Spark (The "Why")
- **The Observation:** In centralized systems, "Time" is an infrastructure service (NTP, Database sequence). In a Sovereign system, the "Global Clock" is the ultimate centralized dependency that must be removed.
- **The Friction:** Realizing that removing the server doesn't just break "Sync"—it breaks the very concept of "Before" and "After." Two devices can never truly agree on what "now" is without a coordinator.
- **The Goal:** Explore the transition from *Chronological Time* (wall clocks) to *Causal Time* (logical order). Explain that conflict isn't a bug to be fixed, but a state to be managed through convergence.

## 2. The Thesis Statement
Synchronization in a decentralized world isn't a technical movement of bits; it is the engineering of a shared history without a boss. Without a central clock, "Now" is no longer a fact—it is a choice made through causal ordering and designed convergence.

## 3. The Target Audience
- **Who:** Developers and Architects exploring Local-First and Decentralized systems.
- **Their State of Mind:** Curious about how "Google Docs without Google" works technically, but perhaps still thinking in terms of "Last Write Wins" or "Server-side timestamps."
- **The Promise:** Shifting their mindset from *Conflict Prevention* (locking) to *Conflict-free Convergence* (CRDTs).
